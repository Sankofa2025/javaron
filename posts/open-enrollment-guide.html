Navigating Health Insurance Open Enrollment: What Illinois Families Need to Know

By Javaron Buckley, Esq.
Published November 1, 2026

The annual health insurance Open Enrollment period is a critical time for individuals and families to review their coverage. While it may seem like just an administrative task, the choices you make can have significant legal implications, especially if you are navigating a divorce, managing a personal injury claim, or updating your estate plan.

For most Illinois residents, the Health Insurance Marketplace Open Enrollment runs from November 1 through January 15. Missing this window could mean being locked into a plan that no longer serves your needs—or going without coverage entirely. Here is how Open Enrollment intersects with your legal rights and responsibilities.

1. Divorce, Child Support, and Dependents

If you are recently divorced or in the process of separating, Open Enrollment is the time to ensure your court orders align with reality. Illinois divorce decrees typically specify which parent is responsible for providing health insurance for the children.

If the designated parent is losing employer-sponsored coverage, they may need to explore COBRA or Marketplace options. Furthermore, if you are claiming a child as a dependent on your taxes, ensure that the health insurance arrangement reflects the custody and support agreements finalized in your divorce decree. Failure to maintain agreed-upon coverage can result in legal enforcement actions.

2. Personal Injury Claims and Health Insurance

If you were injured in an accident this year, your health insurance plays a vital role in your recovery and any potential personal injury claim. Health insurers often have subrogation rights, meaning they may seek reimbursement from your accident settlement for the medical bills they paid.

During Open Enrollment, review your new plan’s deductibles, out-of-pocket maximums, and provider networks. Choosing a plan with better coverage can reduce your immediate financial burden while your personal injury case is pending, and it ensures you are seeing in-network doctors, which simplifies the eventual settlement process.

3. Updating Beneficiaries and Estate Plans

Open Enrollment isn't just about medical coverage; it is also about updating your beneficiary designations. If you have experienced a major life event this year—such as a marriage, divorce, or the birth of a child—your current health insurance beneficiary may be outdated.

While changing a beneficiary on a health plan does not replace a formal will or trust, it is a crucial piece of your broader estate planning puzzle. Ensure your legal documents and your insurance policies are speaking the same language.
