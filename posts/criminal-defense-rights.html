Understanding Your Rights: A Guide to Criminal Defense in Illinois

By Javaron Buckley, Esq.
Published October 30, 2026

Navigating the criminal justice system can be one of the most overwhelming experiences an individual can face. Whether you are under investigation or have already been charged, understanding your fundamental rights is the first step toward protecting your future.

At Sankofa Law Group, we believe that informed clients make better decisions. The United States Constitution provides powerful protections for individuals accused of crimes, and knowing how to exercise them can drastically change the outcome of your case.

The Foundation of Your Defense

Two of the most critical rights to remember during any interaction with law enforcement are your right to remain silent and your right to an attorney. You are never legally obligated to answer questions from law enforcement without counsel present, and anything you say can be used against you in court.

The Sankofa Approach to Defense

Our firm is guided by the Akan principle of Sankofa—the idea that we must look to the past to build a stronger future. In criminal defense, this means conducting a meticulous, backward-looking investigation into the facts of your case. We examine:

  • Police reports and body camera footage for inconsistencies.
  • Witness statements and procedural history for constitutional violations.
  • Mitigating factors that the prosecution may have overlooked.

Why Early Intervention Matters

The most common mistake individuals make is waiting until after an indictment or arrest to seek legal counsel. Early intervention allows an experienced attorney to prevent self-incrimination, preserve crucial evidence before it is lost, and negotiate with prosecutors before formal charges are even filed.

What You Should Take Away

If you or a loved one are facing criminal accusations in the Chicagoland area, do not wait. Time is often the most critical factor in a criminal defense case. Protect your rights by speaking with a qualified attorney before making any statements to law enforcement.
